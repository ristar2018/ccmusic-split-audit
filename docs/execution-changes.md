# Execution-change list

**Version anchor.** The manuscript sources, the code and the result artifacts in this repository correspond to commit `f49030b`. The sentence in the manuscript's data-availability statement that points here resolves to that commit. Commits after it change only this anchor and files regenerated by the release-packaging step.

This is the append-only record of decisions taken **during** execution that were not fixed by `design-plan.md`, plus the values that were settled at execution time. Nothing here changes a pre-specified confirmatory parameter (SESOI, confirmatory family, class universe, backbone pair); those were frozen before the confirmatory results were inspected.

## 1. Interpretation rulings

| id | conflict | ruling |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| J1 | what "equal `n_c`" means for A1 | equality is imposed on the C-side recording count; both A0 and A1 use the full C side |
| J2 | Q-draw noise versus the 2% tolerance | capacity balancing: over 400 random subsets, keep the one minimising `abs(sum(floor(k/2)) - budget)` |
| J3 | the ±2% tolerance applied to α | moved to the `n_c` side (`A1_n_c_side_match`); α is only gated on "realised pool ≥ qp_p5" and is disclosed per class |
| J5 | `m_c = 0.60·S_c` does not fit on the C side | cap at the C side and record `m_c_capped` explicitly (no class triggered the cap in this batch) |
| J6/J7 | small classes cannot reach the α target | structurally infeasible classes are listed separately as `alpha_infeasible` (35 classes) and are not mixed into hard failures |
| P3-9.1 | probe evaluation set | probe is evaluated on an 8:2 split of the *training* side; on instrument-held-out folds the probe is undefined |
| P3-9.3 | adversarial strength | λ capped at 0.02 with a two-epoch warm-up; λ = 0.1 is reported as a representation collapse, not as a tuning result |

## 2. Values settled at execution time

Three values were fixed while the pipeline was being built. The ones listed here are the ones the manuscript and the artifacts use:

1. the α tolerance is implemented on the `n_c` side rather than as a hard gate on α (see J3);
2. the per-class counts in the cross-instrument section are 191 and 167;
3. the GZ_IsoTech slice-count distribution is `{1: 2778, 2: 26, 3: 11, 4: 9}`.

## 3. Negative controls

Six control contrasts (three libraries × two backbones) were run at seed 0. Five had a 95% CI spanning zero. The single exception (chest_falsetto, Swin-T, +5.57) was re-run at three seeds and moved to −2.53 [−4.97, −0.19] with a 16.12-point seed spread. The manuscript therefore reports "five of six intervals span zero, and the sixth returns to within the interval once three seeds are used", and states that chest_falsetto has only four classes, 1,280 slices and 257 test slices, so a single-seed Δ carries no discriminating power there.

## 4. Cross-instrument revisions

Two changes were made to the cross-instrument stage after the first run:

* the probe was moved to the training side (§1, P3-9.1), because a probe evaluated on an instrument-held-out fold has no defined notion of "seen instrument";
* the adversarial term was capped at λ = 0.02 with a warm-up (P3-9.3), after λ = 0.1 collapsed the representation (pooled score 0.2080). No third λ was tried.

Both changes are disclosed in the manuscript, together with the probe AUC/top-1 values, so that the negative result on the adversarial term can be read as "the mechanism did not take effect" rather than "the mechanism took effect and did not help".

## 5. Things deliberately not claimed

* No study-wide GPU-hour total is claimed: timing comes from integer-minute driver logs that are incomplete and rounded.
* No claim is made about the audited authors' actual split procedure; the audit reconstructs one slice-level reading of the stated ratios.
* A2's self-test score is illustrative, not a reproduction of the original benchmark score.

## 6. Prose-to-artifact binding

The number verifier checks that every printed value exists somewhere in the
manuscript; it cannot check that a value is attached to the right qualifier.
One revision failed exactly that way, printing the bel canto recording-level
baseline as "low 0.9s" while the artifact cell held 0.83, so a second gate was
added. `data/results/claim_ledger.csv` binds 88 quantitative prose claims to the
cell they come from, and `code/verify_claim_provenance.py` fails if the cell no
longer evaluates to the printed value, if the phrase leaves its section, or if
the qualifier leaves the sentence. `code/negtest_claim_provenance.py` reverts
three wordings in a temporary copy and requires the expected claims to fail, so
the gate is checked against its own failure modes.

Building the ledger exposed one artifact gap. The ten rows of the headroom table
existed only in the manuscript source, with no artifact behind them;
`code/headroom_table.py` now re-derives them from `e1_metrics.csv` (mean of the
three A0 seeds) and `e1_delta.csv`, and the ten re-derived rows match the table.

One pair of numbers is worth stating explicitly, because the manuscript prints
both and they differ by one. Section 5 describes the budget: remove 4,177 slices
and replace them classwise with 1,665 slices from unused C content and 2,512
from R, which sums to 4,177. Supplement Table 1 reports the realised count:
4,178 pool slices are actually present in A1 training, which is the row count of
`data/splits/v2/CTIS_qpool.csv`. The two figures answer different questions (what
the per-class budget allocates, and what the realised pool contains), and the
packaging manifest quotes the second. The companion counts in the same table,
753 injected recordings and 578 of 963 exposed test recordings, were recomputed
from `CTIS_qpool.csv` and `CTIS_qtest.csv` and match.

## 7. Corrections made in response to human review

A reader report on the submitted draft raised 25 points. Nineteen were accepted
as stated, five were accepted with a correction to the premise, one was accepted
in part, and one was rejected with evidence; the assessment and the disposition
of each point are recorded in the project tree under
`report/审稿意见/human-2026-11-12/`. The changes that touch a reported number or
a factual attribution are listed here.

* **Abstract.** The journal requires a structured abstract of 150--250 words.
  The submitted draft was 338 words and had no structure; it is now 239 words in
  four labelled parts, with no change to any reported value.
* **Slice count in CTIS.** Section 4 now states both counts and why they differ:
  the minimum-recording rule leaves 42,509 of the 43,054 released slices in 201
  classes, four of those slices repeat a content hash already claimed by the
  sealed panel because the same take is released under two technique names, and
  the partitions built from them carry 42,505. Table 1 reports the partition
  counts, and its caption says so. The 42,505 is the sum of
  `data/splits/v2/CTIS_{c,r,qtest,qpool}.csv`, not an error.
* **FMA split unit.** The submitted draft called the FMA partition
  recording-level. It is artist-level: the release states that an artist filter
  keeps every artist in one set only. Section 2 now says artist filter.
* **Sturm attribution.** Section 2 attributes to Sturm the documented mislabels,
  duplicate takes and the inflated accuracy they produce, and no longer the
  stronger claim that the benchmark's conclusions change.
* **Interval stream.** Table 3 and the class-set comparison in the supplement
  re-draw 2,000 recording-cluster replicates on two independently implemented
  streams, so their bounds differ in the second decimal while the point
  estimates are identical. Both captions now say this instead of implying one
  shared stream.
* **Units.** Figures 1--3 and Table 3 state their scale: macro-F1 on 0--1,
  contrasts and seed ranges in percentage points. The decomposition figure (Figure 3)
  previously plotted macro-F1 multiplied by 100 while the three-arm figure (Figure 2)
  plotted the same quantity on 0--1; the review called the first one "Figure 2".
* **Partition names.** Supplement Section 1.11 now maps its arm labels onto the
  partition names of Section 5.7 (A0 = R-slice, A1 and A2 = L-inst,
  A1r = R-rec), and the cross-instrument paragraph of Section 6.4 names the
  partition whose contrast it reports.
* **Wording of the change log.** The manuscript said "during revision", which
  implies a publicly available earlier version. It now says "after the main
  runs", and the same wording is used in the supplement.

The three gates were re-run after these edits: 338 number checks, 88 ledger rows
and the four negative-test cases all pass, and the two figures were regenerated
from the same artifacts.

## 8. Self-check pass of 2026-11-12

A full read of the compiled manuscript against the artifacts found three errors that no
numeric gate could catch, because in each one the number is right and the sentence
around it is wrong.

1. **Tail probability, typesetting.** The sentence reporting the attainable two-sided
   tail had lost its math delimiters and printed as "2,001$" in the PDF. It now prints
   `$2/2{,}001$`. The value was correct in the source and in the artifact; only the
   delimiters were broken, and a string check on the number cannot see them.
2. **Bel canto ceiling, caption.** The caption of the main-effect table said that in
   bel canto under Swin-T "both arms reach 1.00 at the recording level in every seed".
   That holds for A1 and A2 (1.00 in every seed) but not for A0 (0.7490, 0.8357,
   0.7331; mean 0.7726), and the same row reports a recording-level contrast of +22.74,
   which is exactly 100 x (1 - 0.7726). The sentence exists to explain why the A2-A0
   table repeats that row, so it moved to that table's caption and now names the two
   arms it is about.
3. **Duplicate pairs, count.** The materials section reported "three identical pairs
   --- two takes each released under two technique names, and one take with a
   duplicate-download suffix". The hash sweep and the canonical-fold count both give
   two pairs: one take released under two technique names (C0090) and one take released
   twice with a duplicate-download suffix (L0148), with 4,956 recording identifiers
   folding to 4,954. The count is now two, which also makes the paragraph agree with
   the slice-level sentence three paragraphs earlier that names the same take.

Three further inconsistencies were fixed in wording only; no number changed.

4. The abstract and Section 6.1 now give both joint seed--recording upper bounds
   (+3.74 under AlexNet, +3.14 under Swin-T) rather than the Swin-T value alone, which
   the sensitivity table had reported all along.
5. The cross-instrument table caption now states that contrasts are computed on
   unrounded scores, as the main-effect table already did; 0.8446 - 0.8157 prints as
   2.90 because the artifact value is 2.898.
6. The main-effect caption no longer ends on a clause that reads as a claim that both
   CTIS intervals exclude three points. A point estimate below the threshold does not
   imply an interval that excludes it, and the sentence now says so.

Redundancy removed, about 50 words in total: a Section 6.3 sentence that restated the
six-contrast count, the finite-resolution note repeated at the end of the first results
paragraph, the second statement of "no model, epoch or seed selection" in Section 5.3,
the second of two consecutive warnings against reading the cross-instrument gaps as
leakage in Section 6.4, and the parsing log phrase "110,415 of 110,415" in Section 4.2.
The supplement's fold table now says why its two recording columns sum to 1,296 rather
than 1,388 --- the 92 validation recordings of the 90/10 training-side split, 491
slices in the first fold --- and its fold sentence no longer attaches the number 135 to
the merged instrument names.

`code/verify_paper_numbers.py` changed in one place: the cross-instrument universe check
now matches the reworded sentence ("43 techniques over 1,388 recordings, out of 49

The three gates were re-run after these edits: 338 number checks, 88 ledger rows and the

## 9. A third gate for quantified claims

The two gates above compare numbers. Neither can see a sentence whose numbers are right
and whose quantifier is wrong, and two such sentences surfaced while this gate was being
written.

1. **Table 5, ratio range.** The caption said A2's realised training count "is
   0.991--1.001 of A0's in every library". The five ratios are 0.9906 (CTIS), 0.9997
   (music_genre), 0.9965 (pianos), 1.0012 (CNPM) and 1.0014 (bel_canto), so the stated
   interval excludes both ends. It now reads "within one percent of A0's in every
   library", which holds with the largest deviation at 0.94 per cent. Neither verifier
   nor the claim ledger contained the string 0.991, so no earlier check had ever looked
   at this number.
2. **Table 6, "only negative departure".** The caption and the Section 6.5 paragraph
   called the single cell whose interval excludes zero "the only negative departure".
   Four of the six point estimates are negative (-1.37, -2.53, -0.22 and -0.49); only
   one interval excludes zero. Both sentences now say "the only interval that excludes
   zero".

`code/verify_quantifier_claims.py` works in two layers. The claim layer reads
`data/results/quantifier_ledger.csv`, ten rows of id, file, claim, sentence and params.
The sentence has to occur verbatim, after removing whitespace, braces and math
delimiters, in the named source file; the predicate named in the claim column is then
recomputed from the artifacts and compared with params, which also accepts `_le` and
`_ge` inequality keys and `_1dp` / `_2dp` rounding keys. The coverage layer walks every
table float in the manuscript and the supplement and requires each caption containing
both, every, all, each, none, neither, no or only to be covered by a ledger row or by a
waiver. Two captions are waived: "all test recordings" defines the exposure ratio that
the numeric gate already recomputes, and "shown only to document the change" qualifies
the purpose of a column rather than a claim over arms. Figure captions are out of scope;
one of them quantifies, and it describes a drawing rather than a table.

Expected output:

    quantifier claims: 10 in the ledger, 10 true and present, 0 failed
    table captions with a quantifier: 8 checked, 2 waived
    figure captions with a quantifier: 1, out of scope (drawings, not tables)

`code/negtest_quantifier_claims.py` breaks one thing per case --- it drops an
additional-library row so that "every additional-library point estimate does" becomes
false, pushes one A1 recording-level score off the ceiling, rewords a checked sentence,
and adds a table caption with a quantifier but no ledger row --- and requires the gate to
fail with the expected kind and claim id. The clean-tree control must stay green.

Running the gates inside this repository also showed that the shipped
`data/results/paper_verify.json` still recorded 337 passes while the README and the
live run report 338; the file has been regenerated.

The four gates were re-run after these edits: 338 number checks, 88 ledger rows, 10
quantifier claims and nine negative-test cases (four on the claim ledger, five on the
quantifier ledger) all pass. Page counts are unchanged at 31 / 10 / 1, and the compile
log carries no overfull box, undefined reference or missing character.

## 10. The reference list

All 29 entries were checked against the publisher record: 20 through
`api.crossref.org/works/{doi}` field by field, 9 through the publisher page, because
PMLR, JMLR, ISMIR, SMC and ICLR do not register DOIs. Nothing was fabricated. The
per-entry table is in `docs/reference-audit.md`.

Six corrections came out of it. `engel2017` had Eck and Simonyan ahead of Norouzi in
the author list; the printed list truncates after three authors, so no reader could
have seen it. `defferrard2016` and `lee2017` had no pages (316-323, 220-226).
`won2020` carried a page range that no source confirms, and it was removed rather
than left standing. `ganin2015` was missing its issue number (17(59):1-35).
`engel2017`, `ganin2014` and `recht2019` were the only entries printing a `URL` line.

One formatting defect had a mechanical cause worth recording. Sixteen `pages` fields
held a Unicode en dash rather than `--`. `format.pages` in `sn-basic.bst` calls
`multi.page.check`, which tests for an ASCII hyphen, so seven proceedings entries
were printing `p 336-340` for a range while hand-written entries printed `pp`. The
dashes are normalised and the prefixes now agree.

Two things could not be fixed from the `.bib`. `@incollection` page ranges always
print `p`: `bbl.incoll.pages` is hard-coded to `p` at line 273 of the style file and
`format.incoll.pages` uses it for ranges as well, so `downie2010mirex` prints
`p 93-115` although the chapter spans 23 pages. And alphabetical numbering is the
style's own order, set by `SORT` in `presort`; a first citation numbered [13] is not
an error.

Two references were added. The manuscript builds a recording-cluster bootstrap in 20
places and applies Holm's correction in three, and cited neither: Field and Welsh
(2007), *Bootstrapping clustered data*, JRSS-B 69(3):369-390, and Holm (1979), *A
simple sequentially rejective multiple test procedure*, Scandinavian Journal of
Statistics 6(2):65-70. Both are cited at their first occurrence in Section 5. The
extra entries pushed the last one onto a page of its own, so `\bibsep` is set to
0.75em against the class default of 1em, which keeps the list on 31 pages.

The four gates were re-run afterwards and are unchanged: 338 number checks, 88 ledger
rows, 10 quantifier claims, nine negative-test cases. Page counts are 31 / 10 / 1 and
the compile log has no overfull box, no undefined reference and no missing character.
