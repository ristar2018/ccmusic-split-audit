# human_review / selftest.csv

A2 self-test scores of this audit, set beside the F1 values printed in the version of
record of the audited framework. Generated by `code/human_review_selftest.py`:

```
python code/human_review_selftest.py
```

The script exits 1 if any library x backbone cell does not hold exactly three seeds;
that check is the point of it, the rest is arithmetic.

**Read the last three sections before quoting any number here.** The comparison is
contextual. It is not a validation of the reconstruction, and no cell in this table
supports the claim that arm A2 reproduces the published protocol.

line and the right table's label in the middle of it. The pairing was therefore verified
row by row against the typeset table, not against the extracted text alone: Table 7's
AlexNet row is `0.742 0.744 0.542`, and Table 8's `Swin-T 0.906 0.863 0.731` and
`AlexNet 0.919 0.920 0.746` are the right-hand block. The published Table 7 has no
Swin-T at all.

Five of the ten cells have no published counterpart and are left blank with the reason
in `note`; they are not interpolated from a neighbouring library or the other backbone.

## Label spaces, as verified against the version of record

- **CTIS** — instrument recognition, **219 labels**: "The filtered dataset contains
  recordings of 209 types of Chinese traditional musical instruments. Of the original
  287 instrument types, 78 were removed owing to missing instrument labels. Among the
  remaining instruments, seven have two variants each, and one instrument, Yangqin, has
  four variants. We treat variants as separate classes, resulting in a total of 219
  labels." (§3.1.2, printed p.26.) This audit keeps **201 of the 219** after the
  minimum-recording rule.
- **CNPM** — mode classification, **five pentatonic modes** (Gong, Shang, Jue, Zhi, Yu),
  "The total recording number is 287" (§3.5.1, printed p.30). This audit keeps 5 classes
  and 286 of 287 recordings; the class names on disk are anonymised `pattern_0…4`.
- **bel_canto** — singing style classification, **four styles**: bel canto male/female
  and Chinese folk singing male/female (§3.6; the smallest label, Bel Canto Male, holds
  32 clips = 15.8%). This audit keeps 4 classes and 202 of 203 recordings.
- **music_genre**, **pianos** — not CCMusic datasets; the paper publishes no result table
  for either, so no reported value exists to compare against.

## What the published numbers actually measure — and what is unknown

The paper says only this (printed p.26, immediately after the audio-clip count): "we
randomly split the dataset into training, validation, and testing sets at a ratio of
8:1:1". It **names no grouping constraint** anywhere and **does not publish the split**.
So:

- the protocol is stated at 8:1:1 over audio clips; the unit is not declared;
- which particular slices landed on which side is unknown and unrecoverable;
- therefore the reported numbers cannot be verified as having been computed under any
  particular split, and arm A2 here cannot be shown to replay their split. A2 is this
  audit's own reconstruction of the *stated* protocol — same stated ratio, different
  actual slice assignment.

Every row compares "their number under their stated protocol" with "our number under our
reconstruction". The offsets in `reported_minus_selftest` are the difference between two
independently drawn splits plus any label-space difference; they are not a measurement
of protocol agreement.

## Limits — do not read this table as a validation

- **Point estimates only.** Three seeds per cell, spread given as sample SD; no interval
  on the self-test score itself, no seed-level pairing with the published value.
- **`a0_slice` is not an effect size.** It is the recording-disjoint control arm scored on
  a different panel from the self-test arm, so `selftest_minus_a0` mixes arm and panel.
  It is listed for context only; it is not a paired contrast and must not be reported as
  the exposure effect of the published protocol. The paired effect estimates for this
  audit live in `data/results/e1_delta.csv`.
- **One direction, unexplained in part.** Our self-test sits 1.2 to 6.9 points above the
  published value in all five comparable cells (three of them by exactly 2.9). A
  plausible contributor is the class universe (219 published vs 201 audited for CTIS:
  dropping classes with few recordings makes the task easier). That is a hypothesis, not
  a measured correction, and it does not explain bel_canto's −6.9 for Swin-T.
- **Label spaces match in kind but not in size.** CTIS loses 18 classes to the minimum
  recording rule; CNPM and bel_canto keep their full class sets but drop one recording
  each. Class-count differences change macro-F1 independently of any protocol effect.
